# Foreign Aid and Female Education in Developing Countries

## Overview
This repository presents a panel data econometric analysis examining the relationship between foreign aid composition and female education outcomes across 52 developing countries, including Least Developed Countries (LDCs), Lower-Middle-Income Countries (LMICs), and Middle-Income Countries (MICs).

The study focuses on the differential impact of sectoral aid allocation on female literacy rates, with particular emphasis on heterogeneity across income classifications.

## Research Objective
The primary objective of this study is to assess how different categories of foreign aid—specifically education, health, and infrastructure aid—affect female educational outcomes in developing countries. The analysis further investigates whether these effects vary across different income groups.

## Data Description
A balanced panel dataset covering 52 developing countries is constructed for the empirical analysis. The dataset includes countries classified as LDCs, LMICs, and MICs.

### Key Variables
- Female literacy rate (dependent variable)
- Education aid
- Health aid
- Infrastructure aid
- Public expenditure on education
- Mortality rates

## Methodology
The empirical strategy employs panel data econometric techniques using fixed-effects models to control for unobserved country-specific heterogeneity.

### Analytical Approach
- Fixed-effects regression analysis
- Cross-country comparative framework
- Income-group specific estimations

### Software
- STATA
- R


## Key Findings
- Education aid does not exhibit a statistically significant effect on female literacy in LICs and LMICs, and shows a negative association in MICs.
- Health aid demonstrates a strong and statistically significant positive association with female literacy in LICs and LMICs.
- Infrastructure aid is negatively associated with female literacy in LICs, with no statistically significant effects in LMICs or MICs.
- Public expenditure on education shows positive effects in MICs, while effects are statistically insignificant in lower-income groups.
- Mortality rates are negatively associated with female literacy in LICs and LMICs, while a positive association is observed in MICs.


## Contribution
This study contributes to the empirical literature on aid effectiveness by demonstrating that the impact of foreign aid on female education is heterogeneous across income groups and depends significantly on sectoral allocation rather than aggregate aid flows.

The findings provide policy-relevant insights for optimizing aid allocation strategies to improve gendered educational outcomes in developing economies.
